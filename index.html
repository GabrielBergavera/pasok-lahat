PASOKLAHAT!

Different identities. Equal rights. No one left out.

There’s room for everyone. Regardless of who you are, how you identify, or how you express yourself, you deserve respect, equality, and opportunities.

What does “pasok” mean? In Filipino it means “come in,” and also “fit.” This campaign is about making sure everyone fits in a society that has room for all of us. Equality isn’t exclusive.

This is an educational advocacy project that aims to shed light on gender equality, LGBTQIA+ rights, women’s rights, and Philippine laws that uphold the rights and protection of individuals. Our group hopes to provoke thought, raise awareness, and encourage meaningful discussions about these issues that affect our everyday lives, regardless of our gender or identity. After all, a more equal world begins with how we see, respect, and treat one another.

01

Gender-based violence & harassment

What it means

Gender-based violence is harm directed at a person because of their gender or because they don’t meet gender expectations. It can be physical, sexual, emotional, or economic. It is never the victim’s fault.

Forms it can take

  • Unwelcome sexual comments, catcalling, or jokes
  • Stalking or unwanted contact
  • Unwanted touching or sexual advances
  • Pressure or coercion for sexual acts
  • Controlling behavior, threats, or intimidation
  • Sexual assault and other physical violence

How it affects people

Survivors may feel fear, anxiety, shame, or loss of confidence. Some withdraw from school, work, or friends, and some develop health problems. Harassment also teaches others that speaking up is risky.

Consent, boundaries, respect

Consent must be freely given, clear, and specific, and it can be taken back anytime. Silence, fear, or being unable to say no is not consent. Boundaries are personal limits, and everyone gets to set theirs.

🚩 Red flags

  • Ignores “no” or keeps pushing
  • Comments on your body after you’ve asked them to stop
  • Uses power, grades, or a job to pressure you
  • Makes you feel guilty for having boundaries
  • Tracks or controls where you go and who you talk to

✅ Green flags

  • Asks first and accepts “no” without anger
  • Respects privacy and personal space
  • Treats you the same in public and private
  • Welcomes different opinions and identities
  • Makes you feel safe, not small
02

Online abuse

What it looks like

  • Online harassment: repeated unwanted messages or comments
  • Cyberbullying: mocking, shaming, or spreading rumors online
  • Gender-based online abuse: sexist, homophobic, or transphobic attacks
  • Sharing private images without consent
  • Threats and intimidation: messages meant to scare or control

Responsible digital behavior

  • Ask before posting or sharing anyone’s photo.
  • Never forward private images. Stop the chain.
  • Call out hateful comments or report them.
  • Block and report abusive accounts.
  • Keep screenshots as evidence and tell someone you trust.
  • Pause before you post. Would it hurt if it were about you?

04

Reporting & support

If it’s happening to you

  • Get to a safe place first.
  • Tell someone you trust: a friend, family member, teacher, or counselor.
  • Write down what happened, when, and where, if you can do so safely.
  • Report through official channels. You decide what feels right for you.

If you witness it

  • Check on the person, privately and kindly.
  • Listen and believe. Don’t blame.
  • Offer to go with them to report, if they want.
  • Respect their privacy. Never share their story without consent.

School-based support

If you experience or witness discrimination, harassment, sexual harassment, or any situation that requires attention, report it immediately to the appropriate school authority, such as the Dean of Student Affairs Office or the Gender and Development (GAD) Center. Ask the office how reports are handled and what support is available, since procedures differ between schools.
